# f00 collective — non-profit formation (working notes)

**Not legal advice.** This is an ops sketch so the collective can receive
and pass support for open art/tech work without becoming a company.
Talk to a lawyer who knows exempt orgs (and, if needed, state nonprofit
formation) before filing anything.

## Why bother

- Attract **project funding** (grants, gifts) into a **shared pool**.
- Issue receipts / report cleanly if donors care about formal status.
- Stay consistent with f00 law: **MIT, open, no paywalls, no owners,
  one pool, even split among stewards, no gods.**

Why *not* rush:

- A **501(c)(3)** is paperwork with the state and IRS — tension with
  “out of the mall,” but it is the usual US path for **tax-deductible**
  gifts and many foundation grants.
- Form without discipline and you get a **company with softer language**.

## Fit check (f00 constraints)

| Constraint | Implication |
|------------|-------------|
| Art + tech + open tools | Purpose language: **education, arts, science** — not “sell software” |
| MIT / free forever | Compatible with public benefit; avoid exclusive licenses |
| Fund projects | Prefer **grants to stewards/projects** with open deliverables |
| No owners / no leads | Bylaws: **stewards**, one person one vote, no super-vote class |
| Even-split gifts | Policy doc: unrestricted gifts → pool → split or project grants |
| No conglomerate rails | Can still take XMR/cash; formal org may *also* open a bank later |

## Paths (pick one to start)

### A. Fiscal sponsorship (fastest, least identity load)

Partner with an existing 501(c)(3) that sponsors art/tech projects
(fiscal host). They hold the tax ID; f00 is a **sponsored project**.

- **Pros:** Live in months not years; deductible gifts sooner; less board
  theater.
- **Cons:** Host fee %; their rules; less “our” entity.
- **Use when:** You want to fund projects **now** without building a
  shell.

### B. Own 501(c)(3) — public charity (arts / education / science)

Form a **state nonprofit corporation** (or unincorporated association →
corp), adopt bylaws, apply for **federal tax exemption** under 501(c)(3).

Typical exempt framing for f00:

- **Educational / scientific:** open-source tools, documentation, public
  research.
- **Literary / artistic:** zines, visual work, film, sound under the
  collective’s aesthetic and open license.

- **Pros:** Control; grant eligibility; durable pool; can make **project
  grants**.
- **Cons:** Form 1023 (or 1023-EZ if small); annual 990; board duties;
  private inurement rules (no private profit to insiders).

**Bylaws must encode f00 politics in plain legal clothes:**

- No “CEO / president for life” cosplay — rotate officers if required,
  but **decision power for admissions stays one person one vote** among
  stewards of released projects.
- **No ownership** of project IP beyond MIT (or equally free) license.
- **Dissolution:** assets to another open/art/education org, not private
  pockets.

### C. Unincorporated association + diy only (purest, weakest funding)

Stay informal; XMR/cash only; no tax receipts.

- **Pros:** Matches anti-corp gut.
- **Cons:** Hard to take serious grants; personal liability risk; donors
  who need 501c3 walk away.

### D. Cooperative / mutual (different tool)

Worker or multi-stakeholder co-op is **not** the same as charity. Useful
for revenue-shared labor, wrong for “tax-deductible art fund.” Can exist
*beside* a 501c3, not instead of gift rails.

## Recommended sequence for f00

1. **Keep diy give live** (`f00.sh/donate` — XMR/cash on request).
2. **Document voters/stewards** quietly (person → contact → projects).
3. **Proposal → open vote → land** process (GitHub issue ledger).
4. **Fiscal sponsor pilot** for one funded project (learn the rails).
5. If the collective still wants its own shell: **state nonprofit +
   1023** with arts/education purpose and f00 bylaws.
6. Only then: bank account, optional Stripe *as last resort*, still
   prefer XMR/cash/local.

## Formation checklist (own 501c3)

- [ ] Purpose statement (arts + open tech education; public benefit)
- [ ] Name / DBA availability in home state
- [ ] Incorporators + initial board (need people who accept duty)
- [ ] Articles of incorporation (state)
- [ ] Bylaws: stewards, votes, no owners, MIT/open mandate, dissolution
- [ ] Conflict-of-interest policy
- [ ] EIN (IRS)
- [ ] Form 1023 or 1023-EZ
- [ ] State charity registration if soliciting donations in-state
- [ ] Bank (when ready) + bookkeeping + annual 990
- [ ] Grant policy: who can apply, open deliverables, no private inurement
- [ ] Public donate page still one pool → f00.sh/donate

## What the pool is for (program spend)

Gifts and any physical-sales surplus go back into **more released work**,
not private income:

| Kind | Examples |
|------|----------|
| Sound | Recording, masters, pressing, free digital release |
| Print | Art books, magazines, zines, paper, ink, postage |
| Image / object | Prints, small editions (mission-related) |
| Tech | Servers, bandwidth, domains, open-source infra |
| Materials | Artist supplies when they become the next project |

### Free digital · physical for cost recovery

- **Digital:** free forever (code MIT; art/music files free to take where
  practical). No paywall on the cultural object.
- **Physical:** may be **sold at cost or modest surplus** only if surplus
  returns to the **same collective pool** to fund the next release.
- **No profit distributions** to stewards, founders, or “owners.”
- **No one gets rich**; the table gets larger.

This is the “cycle”: give / cost-sale → pool → next album, next zine,
next server, next open tool.

### Tax note (US 501c3, talk to counsel)

- Mission-related sales (e.g. zines that *are* the educational/artistic
  program) are often fine; pure merch for logo cash can trip
  **unrelated business income (UBIT)**.
- Keep books: gifts vs program revenue; document that surplus funds
  further exempt purpose work.
- Grants to projects: open deliverables, public report, no private
  inurement.

## Granting to projects (once funded)

- Written application (fits hub + mantra + open license).
- Collective vote if required by bylaws.
- **Open MIT (or freer) for code; free digital for media** required.
- Budget: production costs, not lifestyle.
- Report: what shipped, where public, how free digital is obtained.
- Money is **support for work**, not purchase of ownership.

## Anti-patterns

- Per-project tip jars (re-creates fiefs).
- “Founder” super-vote.
- Closed source “for sustainability.”
- Paywall “members area.”
- Discord as the only vote record.
- Becoming a pass-through for one person’s lifestyle.

## Spin-out idea (later f00 project)

A small **formation + grant desk** tool (Workers + D1): proposals,
steward roll, open ballots, grant ledger — MIT, self-hosted aesthetic.
**Not first.** Human process first; software only when paper hurts.
