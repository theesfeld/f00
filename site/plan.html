Toward a public-benefit shell.

This is our open plan for receiving and passing support without becoming a shop. We are not a registered non-profit or 501(c)(3) today. Gifts are not tax-deductible. We are trying to become a formal public-benefit body that still obeys the constitution. When status is real, we will say so clearly here and on donate.

plain text: plan.md · this is not legal advice

Why bother

So gifts and grants can land in a shared pool that funds open work — tools, albums, books, zines, film, sound, machines — without private books or a founder tip jar. So we can report cleanly if a donor needs formal structure. So the culture survives contact with money.

Why not rush: paperwork is tension with “out of the mall.” Form without discipline and you get a company with softer language. We would rather stay pure and slow than claim a costume.

What must not break

Whatever shell we choose, the constitution still binds practice:

  • collective, not vendor — stewards, no owners / heads / leads
  • one person, one vote among stewards of released projects
  • MIT (or freer) for code; free digital for media
  • one pool; open ledger; no payroll default
  • physical sales only to recycle cost into more work
  • no claim of charity status until true and published
  • never dissolve for private profit — residual only to other 501(c)(3)s

If the shell ends

Bylaws and articles must say this in hard language: the nonprofit cannot be dissolved for profit of any kind. No founder take. No equity residual. After debts, either transfer remaining assets to another 501(c)(3), or liquidate and donate all remaining funds to other nonprofits that qualify. Preferred residual homes (list open; more to be decided): Sisters of the Road, Food Not Bombs (or a 501(c)(3) host for that mission), plus others we name in the open before any wind-down. Court fallback still only for public-charity purposes — never private pockets.

Paths we are weighing

A · Fiscal sponsorship. Partner with an existing public charity that hosts projects. Faster deductible gifts for some donors; their framework; less “our” entity. Useful as a pilot while we learn rails.

B · Own public-benefit corporation + federal exemption. State formation, governing documents, federal application for tax exemption under the usual US charity path (often described as 501(c)(3) when granted). Control and durability; more duty and annual reporting. Only claim after the determination is real.

C · Stay informal + diy only. Cash, Monero, open ledger, no tax receipts. Purest gut; weakest for foundation grants; personal liability risk stays higher.

A cooperative is a different tool — useful beside a gift shell, not a substitute for a public-benefit fund.

Sequence we are on

Live practice first. Paper second. Status last — and only when honest.

  1. Keep diy give live at f00.sh/donate (one pool, quiet rails).
  2. Keep the open ledger complete — every gift, expense, sale, transfer.
  3. Hold the steward roll and the constitution in public view.
  4. Run proposal → open vote → land in a place anyone can read later (not a private chat as sole record).
  5. Optionally pilot fiscal sponsorship for one funded project before building our own shell.
  6. If we still want our own entity: state formation, bylaws that encode this culture, conflict-of-interest policy, EIN, federal application, state charity registration where required.
  7. Only after recognition: say so on donate, plan, and ledger — tax language, bank if needed, still prefer diy over mall rails.

Purpose language (draft, for later filing)

When (if) we file, the purpose we aim to state is educational, literary/artistic, and scientific — not “sell software.” In plain terms:

Create, publish, and freely distribute educational and artistic works — software, documentation, music, film, visual art, books, magazines, zines — under open licenses; educate the public about open culture, craft, and technology; provide materials and infrastructure for that production; support related open work through a shared program fund.

Activities already in motion: open-source tools under MIT; free digital media; optional physical editions that recycle cost into more work; public hub and documentation; open financial ledger.

What the pool is for

Program spend only. Examples: recording and pressing; paper, ink, postage; servers, bandwidth, domains; artist materials when they become the next project. Free digital forever where we control the drop. Physical only as cost-cycle, surplus back into the same pool. No profit distributions. No lifestyle pass-through dressed as “support.”

Formation checklist (own shell)

  • purpose statement locked to constitution
  • name / DBA availability in home state
  • incorporators + initial board who accept duty
  • articles of incorporation
  • bylaws: stewards, votes, open mandate, no-profit dissolution
  • dissolution: all residual → other 501(c)(3)s (Sisters of the Road, FNB, … TBD)
  • conflict-of-interest policy
  • EIN
  • federal exemption application (when ready)
  • state charity registration if we solicit there
  • books that reconcile to the public ledger
  • grant / gift policies with open deliverables
  • public pages still say the truth about status

Internal worksheets and templates may live offline for counsel review. Anything that affects the public story belongs here, on the hub, or in git — not in a secret slide deck.

What this plan refuses

Claiming we are a charity before we are. Per-project tip jars. Founder super-votes. Closed source “for sustainability.” Paywalled “members areas.” Discord as the only ballot box. Private books beside a public ledger. Becoming a pass-through for one person’s lifestyle. Corporate matching theater as our personality.

Status right now

Not registered. Collective practice + open ledger + diy give + written constitution. Working toward a formal shell. No tax receipts. Love first.
