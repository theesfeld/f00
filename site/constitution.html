How we bind ourselves.

This is the written bond of the collective — mindset, methodology, and how we decide. It is not a company charter. It is not a list of punishments. It is what we agree to when we put work on the table. The door is who. The path toward a formal public-benefit shell is the plan. This page is the full practice.

plain text: constitution.md

Preamble

f00 is a collective of people who build with machines and with hands — code, print, sound, film, whatever cuts. Not a vendor. Not a brand deck. A room of people who make things that refuse to lie about the world. We do this for love. We project the real world onto the display. We leave the math for the math. We come unfinished.

I · Collective, not corporation

We are not a software company. We are not a membership club with ladders. The face of f00 is projects — tools, zines, albums, film, pages — sitting next to each other without sanding the soul off. People still decide. Titles that rank people over projects do not live here: no owners, heads, leads, masters, or gods. Only stewards of the work they put down.

II · Stewards and the vote

If you steward at least one released project under f00, you hold a vote. One person, one vote — no matter how many projects you already carry. Having one in does not mean the next one walks in. A yes is for that project, that time — not a lifetime pass. A no is not exile. Bring it again when it is sharper, or make something else.

III · How a project lands

Propose. Anyone may bring work — or a sharp outline — that could live on the hub without lying. Send it to tj@f00.sh or @lameserver on X: what it is, why it fits, who would steward it.

Open. The proposal is written into the open record where voters can all see it. Stewards get a clear window to vote yes or no. Votes are by person, recorded in the open. Simple majority of votes cast decides. Chat rooms may exist for talk. They are not the ledger. The vote lives where anyone can read it later.

Land or not. Yes → the project may take a seat on the hub when it has a real release. No → the idea is free; try again later or elsewhere.

IV · Open to the world

Every f00 project is MIT-licensed where it is code, and open to the world either way. Free to read, copy, change, and share. No payments to use the work. Digital drops stay free. Physical things (vinyl, books, zines) may exist only to cover cost and feed the collective pool so more work can ship — not so anyone gets rich. No closed editions dressed as freedom. No paywalls. No “owners” of culture — only stewards of what they put down.

V · Money is bread, not rent

We do not monetize this directly. No funnel. No cup on the sidewalk. We do not beg. We do not panhandle. The practice is quiet: make the thing, put it down, keep walking.

If someone chooses to give, that is allowed — one collective pool only, never per project — and gifts are shared like bread, not a tip jar for a founder. Optional support lives at f00.sh/donate. Every movement of value is on the open ledger ((ledger.json) — no payroll, no private books. If it is not in the ledger, it did not happen.

The pool funds program work: albums, books, zines, servers, domains, materials, postage — more released work. No one takes a profit cut. No equity. Stewards do not get rich; the table gets larger.

VI · Public-benefit shell (aspiration)

We are not a registered public charity or 501(c)(3) today. Do not treat gifts as tax-deductible. We are working toward a formal public-benefit shell so we can receive and pass support without becoming a shop. We will not claim that status until it is true and published here. The living path is the plan. Until then, and after, the work stands on love alone — and this constitution still holds.

VII · Mindset — the world is a projection

Perfection is a math problem. Uniformity only lives in equations. Outside mathematics, nothing is 100% straight, 100% crisp, 100% the same as last time. Pattern without clones. Species you recognize; specimens you never confuse.

Order lives inside disorder. Chaos is not a flat smear. A body under skin is organs, not noise. Some edges stay sharp. Some dissolve. Straightness can survive in places — never everywhere at once, and never by force of a single soft filter.

Every object is a throw. Logo, card, letter, field: each is light through a thing, through a lens, onto a screen. Each has its own seat in the tray. Not layout jitter dressed up as life. A view of a natural thing, projected onto the display.

Letterpress, not laser. Hand-set type knows the spacer’s hand. Readable first. Alive second. Both required. We pull the skin open. We show the organs. Perfect anti-uniformity — natural, organic, entropic — because that is the world.

Usable zen. Entropy is not an excuse for mess. The band is narrow on purpose: enough variation that the eye believes the world, never so much that you cannot read, aim, or rest. Beauty that works. Work that looks like it grew.

VIII · Methodology — how we build

Release is the card. Only released work sits on the hub grid. Ideas talk; ships land.

One aesthetic spine. Shared theme from the hub. Project sites get layout, not a new brand hex every week.

Catalog is truth. Project list and links live in one open file; the site is derived, not freelanced into lying.

Open process. Proposals, votes, money, and formation status are public enough to audit. Secret power is a bug.

Discipline over costume. Cutting edge without the cosplay. Anti-corp gut without abandoning honesty about paperwork when we choose it.

IX · Who may walk in

Artists. Coders. Visionaries. Musicians. Bands. Filmmakers. Printers. Whoever cuts — if it is a project you can stand next to. This is not just coding. Code is one tool. So is ink. So is a camera. So is a zine folded wrong on purpose. So is a song that almost holds together.

X · What we refuse

Manufactured perfection. Identical throws. Razor edges as religion. Metronome motion. Flat blur as “organic.” Anything that pretends the screen is more pure than the room it sits in. Company cosplay. Membership ladders. Selling the lifestyle. Owners. Heads. Leads. Masters. Gods. Per-project tip jars. Private books next to a public ledger. Paywalls dressed as community. Closed source “for sustainability.” Discord as the only vote record. Claiming charity status we have not earned.

XI · If we ever end

Any formal shell we form cannot be dissolved for profit of any kind — not for stewards, founders, donors, or anyone else. No residual as equity, dividend, or private take. On dissolution or liquidation, after lawful debts, all remaining funds and property go only to other public charities (organizations described as 501(c)(3) when that is the path), either by transferring the work to another such body or by liquidating and donating every leftover coin. Preferred residual homes (more to be decided in the open): Sisters of the Road, Food Not Bombs (or a qualifying 501(c)(3) host that holds for that mission), and others we name before any wind-down. Never a private pocket. This is non-negotiable in bylaws and articles if we file.

XII · How this document lives

This constitution is public and versioned with the hub. It may change when stewards of released projects agree, in the open, that the bond needs to grow. Change is not fashion. Change is when practice teaches us we were wrong or incomplete. Legal bylaws of any future public-benefit shell must not gut this culture; they may only dress it for the state and the ledger of the state. Culture leads. Paperwork follows — or we do not file. Dissolution-for-profit language is never weakened.
